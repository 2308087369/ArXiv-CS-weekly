## **1. Abstract**

This paper, jointly released by researchers from Tsinghua University, Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University (Ziyu Wang, Yong-Lu Li, etc.), targets the "Achilles' heel" of the current Embodied AI field—**the fragmentation and simplification of evaluation systems**.

With the explosive growth of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, robots seem to have matured in basic tasks like "pick-and-place." However, this study points out that this is more of an illusion caused by "low evaluation standards." To this end, the team proposed **The Great March 100 (GM-100)**, a benchmark test set containing 100 long-tail, detail-oriented tasks. It is not just a set of test questions, but an attempt to build a standardized paradigm for the "Embodied AI Olympics," forcing models to shift from simple "semantic understanding" to deep "physical reasoning."

## **2. The Great March 100 (GM-100) — The "Legislative Moment" for Embodied AI Evaluation**

### **2.1 Background: The "Evaluation Crisis" and "Illusion of Prosperity" in Embodied AI**

In early 2026, the field of embodied intelligence is at a delicate turning point. On one hand, large models represented by Google RT-2, Octo, and OpenVLA have demonstrated amazing semantic generalization capabilities; on the other hand, these robots still appear "clumsy" when facing slightly complex real-world scenarios.

The paper deeply reveals three major pain points of existing benchmarks (such as subsets of Open X-Embodiment):
1.  **Common Task Bias:** About 80% of tasks concentrate on a very few verbs (such as Pick, Place). The 90%+ success rates models achieve on these high-frequency tasks cannot represent their true survival ability in complex environments.
2.  **Long-tail Ignorance:** Real-life operations often involve a large number of low-frequency but critical details. For example, "picking up a cup" is a high-frequency task, but "picking up a thin-walled glass full of hot water while avoiding expensive electronic devices on the table" is a "long-tail" challenge that determines system reliability.
3.  **Lack of Design Principles:** Previous task selection mostly relied on researchers' subjective intuition or existing laboratory equipment, lacking a systematic theoretical support based on **behavioral science** and **object affordance**.

This status quo has led to the failure of "weights and measures": We cannot distinguish whether a model truly understands the physical world or has merely learned to imitate statistical patterns in the dataset.

### **2.2 Deep Analysis of Core Contributions and Methodology**

The core value of "The Great March 100" lies in the **"scientific nature"** of its benchmark construction.

#### **2.2.1 Introducing HAKE: From "Macro Actions" to "Atomic Knowledge"**

To break the limitation of "brainstorming" task design, the research team introduced **HAKE (Human Activity Knowledge Engine)**. This is a human behavior knowledge base long maintained by Cewu Lu's team at Shanghai Jiao Tong University.
*   **Core Logic:** HAKE decomposes complex human activities into **Part-states**. For example, it does not simply define "drinking water," but breaks it down into atomic states such as "hand: holding cup handle," "arm: lifting," "head: tilting slightly back."
*   **Application:** GM-100 uses HAKE's atomic knowledge to semantically expand existing robot tasks. This method ensures the coverage breadth of the 100 tasks in the Action Space, ranging from fine fingertip operations to large-scale limb coordination.

#### **2.2.2 Object Affordance and Micro-geometric Constraints**

Task design no longer focuses solely on "success or failure," but emphasizes **detailed constraints during the process**:
*   **Affordance Perception:** The model must identify specific interaction areas of objects (e.g., spout, handle, switch).
*   **Micro-geometric Constraints:** Many tasks require robots to operate within extremely narrow spaces or maintain specific angles during operation to avoid collisions. This design directly hits the weak point of current VLA models' "insufficient spatial reasoning precision."

#### **2.2.3 Rigorous Selection Process: LLM Scale Generation + Expert Review**

1.  **Semantic Cleaning:** Remove duplicates from existing datasets.
2.  **LLM Expansion:** Use large language models (such as GPT-4o) combined with HAKE prompts to generate thousands of potential long-tail task candidates.
3.  **Hybrid Filtration:** Combined with Hardware Feasibility and data collection difficulty, human experts perform the final check, ultimately selecting 100 tasks.

### **2.3 Experimental Results and Industry Implications: A Tougher "Exam Syllabus"**

The research team collected a large amount of high-quality trajectory data on mainstream robot platforms such as **Cobot Magic** and benchmarked existing top models.

**Key Findings:**
*   **Discriminative Power:** Models that performed excellently under traditional tasks showed a precipitous drop in performance on GM-100 (generally dropping by 30%-50%). This proves that GM-100 can effectively identify the "pseudo-generalization" of models.
*   **Feasibility Verification:** Although the tasks are arduous, experiments proved that human operators can complete them stably, ruling out "physically impossible" tasks.

**Strategic Implications:**
For developers, the release of GM-100 signifies the **end of the "leaderboard chasing era"** and the **beginning of the "detail era"**.
*   **From Scaling Data to Scaling Quality:** Simply piling up simple grasping data is meaningless. Future competitiveness lies in how to make the model perceive the microscopic details of the physical world.
*   **Robustness is Justice:** For robots to enter unstructured environments like homes and factories, they rely not on a 99% average success rate, but on the extreme handling capability for that 1% of long-tail, complex, and highly constrained tasks.
