## 2. Core Architectural Innovation: Fusion of MoE and ZigZag Attention

The foundational architecture design of LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 reflects a pragmatic trend in current large model design: pursuing the ultimate balance between model capacity and inference cost. As a giant model with 560 billion parameters, if a Dense architecture were adopted, its inference cost would be unbearable for most institutions. Therefore, the LongCat team chose the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture as the cornerstone.

### 2.1 Analysis of Sparse Activation MoE Architecture

The total model parameters reach 560B, but when processing each token, only about 27B parameters are activated to participate in the calculation. The logic behind this design is that when language models handle specific tasks (such as code generation, mathematical proof, or creative writing), they do not need the participation of the "whole brain" (all neurons).

| Metric | Parameter Details |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Total Parameters** | 560 Billion |
| **Activated Parameters** | ~27 Billion / token |
| **Architecture Type** | Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) |
| **Inference Latency Benchmark** | Equivalent to ~30B Dense model |

This high Sparsity Ratio design brings two significant advantages:

1.  **Maximization of Knowledge Capacity:** The 560B parameter space allows the model to store massive world knowledge, especially Long-tail Knowledge, which is crucial for handling diverse queries in general domains.
2.  **Controllable Inference Cost:** The 27B activated parameters ensure that the computational load (FLOPs) of a single forward pass remains at a low level, making the model feasible for actual deployment rather than just a laboratory product.

### 2.2 ZigZag Attention: Efficiency Revolution for Long Context

In Agentic AI application scenarios, models often need to process extremely long contexts (Long Context), such as reading entire technical manuals, analyzing massive codebases, or maintaining conversation history spanning several days. The time complexity of the traditional Full Attention mechanism is quadratic to the sequence length ($O(L^2)$), which brings huge computational and memory pressure when processing 128K or even 1M tokens.

LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 introduces the **ZigZag Attention (LoZA)** mechanism to solve this bottleneck.

#### 2.2.1 Core Principle: Reapplication of the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis

The design inspiration for ZigZag Attention comes from the "Lottery Ticket Hypothesis." This hypothesis suggests that within a massive neural network, there exists a smaller sub-network (the winning lottery ticket) that, if trained in isolation, can achieve performance comparable to the original network.

The LoZA mechanism plays a role in the "Mid-training" of the model:

1.  **Identify Redundancy:** The system first identifies those attention layers that can be sparsified without significantly damaging performance in long-context tasks.
2.  **Sparsify & Rewind:** Sparsify these layers and "rewind" the parameters to the state at the beginning of mid-training.
3.  **Retrain:** Under the premise of maintaining the sparse structure, continue pre-training the model to adapt it to long-context processing.

#### 2.2.2 Performance Gains

Through this "Calibrate-Rewind-Retrain" process, ZigZag Attention successfully significantly reduces the computational overhead of long-context processing while maintaining the ability to capture long-range dependencies. According to the report, the LongCat-Flash-Thinking-ZigZag version not only supports ultra-long contexts but also achieves significant acceleration in decoding-intensive tasks such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and Tool-integrated Reasoning. This capability provides the necessary context window support for the subsequent "Heavy Thinking" mode, as deep thinking processes inevitably generate a large number of intermediate Chain-of-Thought.

## 3. Heavy Thinking Mode: The Pinnacle of Test-Time Compute

If the MoE architecture is the "body" of LongCat, then "Heavy Thinking Mode" is its "soul." This is the most disruptive innovation in the report, marking the leap of AI models from "instant reaction" to "deliberation."

### 3.1 Theoretical Basis: Sutton's Bitter Lesson and Inference Scaling Laws

Rich Sutton proposed "The Bitter Lesson," pointing out that leveraging compute is the most effective way to improve AI performance. In the pre-training phase, this computational law has been verified (Scaling Laws). In the inference phase, models like OpenAI o1 have proven that increasing the computational amount during inference (Test-Time Compute) can also significantly improve model performance.

LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 pushes this concept to the extreme, proposing two orthogonal scaling dimensions for inference capability: **Depth and Width**.

### 3.2 Width Scaling: Parallel Thinking

When facing a complex problem (such as a difficult math problem or a complex code refactoring task), human experts often try multiple solution paths. LongCat simulates this process.

*   **Mechanism:** The model does not generate a single chain of thought during inference but concurrently launches multiple independent thinking threads (Parallel Rollouts).
*   **Diversity Guarantee:** To prevent multiple threads from falling into the same thinking trap, the system applies a higher inference temperature (Temperature), forcing the model to explore different regions in the solution space.
*   **Advantage:** This parallel exploration greatly increases the probability of finding the correct solution path, especially in tasks where the solution space is huge and rugged.

### 3.3 Depth Scaling: Iterative Summarization Loop

Generating a large number of parallel ideas is not enough; convergence is also needed. LongCat introduces a summary-based recursive feedback mechanism to achieve the scaling of reasoning depth.

*   **Phase 1:** The model generates multiple preliminary thinking trajectories in parallel.
*   **Phase 2 (Summarization):** A specially trained "Summarization Model" (or the summary mode of the original model) evaluates and synthesizes these trajectories, extracting valuable intermediate conclusions and discarding incorrect paths.
*   **Phase 3 (Recursion):** The summarized information is fed back to the input end as the starting point for the next round of thinking.
*   **Loop:** This process constitutes a closed loop (Iterative Reasoning Loop), allowing the model to perform deeper deduction based on previous thinking results.

### 3.4 Performance Qualitative Change

This "Parallel Exploration + Iterative Convergence" mechanism allows LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 to demonstrate astonishing performance improvements on extremely challenging tasks. The most typical example is in the **AIME-25** mathematics competition benchmark:

*   **Standard Mode:** 99.6%
*   **Heavy Thinking Mode:** **100.0%**

This is not just a score improvement; it symbolizes that the model has actually "solved" mathematical reasoning tasks at this difficulty level. Data shows that as the test-time computational budget increases, the advantage of Heavy Thinking mode becomes increasingly obvious, far exceeding traditional Self-Consistency methods.

## 4. Infrastructure: DORA Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning Framework

Training a 560B parameter model for Reinforcement Learning (RL) is an engineering nightmare. Traditional RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) workflows usually employ synchronous PPO algorithms, which means the entire cluster must wait for the slowest GPU to complete sample generation (Rollout) before gradient updates can be performed. In large-scale clusters, "long-tail latency" caused by hardware failures and network jitter is common, leading to extremely serious waste of computing power.

The LongCat team built the **DORA (Dynamic ORchestration for Asynchronous rollout)** system for this purpose, which is the invisible hero behind its high performance.

### 4.1 Asynchronous Orchestration Architecture

DORA completely decouples the various links in RL training, splitting them into modules that run independently and communicate via high-speed RPC:

1.  **Generation Phase:** Responsible for executing the Policy Model and generating a large number of inference trajectories (Trajectories). These worker nodes (Workers) run asynchronously and do not block each other.
2.  **Experience-Maker Phase:** Receives generated trajectories and computes Rewards and Advantage functions. This step usually requires calling the Reward Model or executing external tools like code interpreters and math verifiers.
3.  **Model Training Phase:** Also known as the Learner, it pulls data from a streaming Experience Buffer for gradient updates.

### 4.2 Solving the "Straggler Problem"

Under the DORA architecture, slow nodes do not block the entire training process. As long as there is enough data in the experience buffer, training nodes can continue to update parameters. The report claims that compared to synchronous methods, DORA achieves a training speed improvement of **over 3x** on the scale of tens of thousands of accelerators.

### 4.3 Scaling Effect

This engineering breakthrough directly translates into improved model performance. Due to improved training efficiency, the LongCat model was able to train in **over 10,000 environments**, covering more than 20 different domains. This extensive environmental interaction is the basis for the model's strong generalization ability.

## 5. Training Methodology: From Cold Start to Domain-Parallel RL

The training process of LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 is precisely designed, divided into multiple stages, each with clear optimization goals.

### 5.1 Long CoT Cold-Start

Before RL intervention, the model first needs to learn "how to think." The LongCat team adopted a carefully designed cold-start strategy:

*   **Data Synthesis:** Use high-capability teacher models or generate long chain-of-thought data through Expert Iteration.
*   **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Fine-tune on these data to enable the model to acquire basic reasoning patterns, format specifications, and logic of thinking jumps.

### 5.2 Domain-Parallel RL

This is a highlight of the LongCat training process. Typically, training general models faces an "Alignment Tax," where improving capability in one domain (e.g., math) may damage capability in another (e.g., creative writing).

To solve this problem, LongCat adopted a **Domain-Parallel** training scheme:

1.  **Decoupled Optimization:** Divide training tasks into independent domains such as **STEM**, **Coding**, and **Agentic**.
2.  **Parallel Expert Training:** Perform reinforcement learning optimization separately for each domain. This means that during training, multiple "domain expert models" evolve in parallel.
3.  **Model Fusion:** After each domain model reaches optimality, merge them into a final model using model fusion technology. This method aims to approximate Pareto-optimal, maximizing performance in all domains without sacrificing others.

### 5.3 Noise Injection and Robustness Training

Addressing the fragility of Agentic AI in real-world environments, the LongCat team implemented a systematic **Noise Injection** strategy.

*   **Noise Analysis:** The team deeply analyzed failure modes of real-world API calls, including network timeouts, parameter errors, abnormal return formats, irrelevant search results, etc.
*   **Curriculum Learning:** Constructed an automated environment generation pipeline to gradually increase the type and intensity of noise during training.
*   **Effect:** The model was forced to learn "defensive thinking." When a tool call fails, it does not crash or hallucinate but tries to consult documentation, modify parameters to retry, or find alternative solutions. This capability was verified in high-difficulty benchmarks like **VitaBench**, where LongCat demonstrated stability significantly superior to baseline models.

## 6. Deep Analysis of Experimental Results

The experimental evaluation of LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 covers multiple dimensions such as mathematical reasoning, code generation, and agent tool use, and the comparison objects are all top models at the time.

### 6.1 Mathematics and Logical Reasoning (AIME-25 & GPQA)

In the field of mathematical reasoning, the model demonstrated dominant performance.

| Benchmark | DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking | LongCat-Flash-Thinking | LongCat (Heavy Mode) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **AIME-25 (Avg@16)** | 93.5% | 99.6% | **100.0%** |
| **HMMT-25 (Avg@16)** | 93.5% | 93.4% | **97.5%** |
| **GPQA-Diamond** | 86.9% | 80.5% | **85.2%** |


It can be seen from the table that in standard Thinking mode, LongCat is already significantly ahead of DeepSeek-V3.2 (99.6% vs 93.5%). With Heavy Thinking mode enabled, AIME-25 reached a perfect score. This indicates that for well-defined logical problems, LongCat's deep reasoning capability has touched the ceiling of existing evaluation systems.

### 6.2 Agent and Tool Use (Agentic Benchmarks)

This part is LongCat's biggest differentiated advantage compared to other reasoning models.

*   **AIME-25 Token Efficiency:** While maintaining high accuracy, the average Token consumption was reduced by **64.5%**. This means the model not only thinks more accurately but also "smarter," avoiding invalid thinking loops.
*   **BrowseComp (Web Browsing):** Scored **73.1%**, demonstrating strong web information extraction and navigation capabilities.
*   **RWSearch (Real-World Search):** Scored **77.7%**.
*   **VitaBench:** Scored **29.3%**. Although the absolute score seems low, VitaBench is known for its extreme difficulty, with many models scoring close to zero. LongCat's score establishes its leading position in handling complex multimodal, multi-step tasks.

### 6.3 Generalization Ability Evaluation

To verify whether the model is just "memorizing questions," the research team built an **Automated Task Synthesis Pipeline**.

*   **Mechanism:** Users input keywords, and the system automatically generates complex tasks containing random toolsets and execution environments.
*   **Result:** In these never-before-seen synthetic tasks, LongCat maintained leading performance, proving the strong generalization ability brought by its domain-parallel RL and noise injection training.

## 7. Discussion: Evolution from Model to System

### 7.1 The Game Between Open Source and Closed Source

The open-source strategy of LongCat-Flash-Thinking-2601 (weights, code, online experience all open) has strong strategic significance. In early 2026, although models like DeepSeek-R1 have been open-sourced, LongCat provides a more complete "Agent-Ready" solution. especially the open-sourcing of Heavy Thinking mode allows the community to glimpse and reproduce advanced reasoning mechanisms similar to OpenAI o1, which will greatly accelerate the iteration of reasoning-side algorithms in the open-source community.

### 7.2 Infrastructure as a Core Moat

Through the analysis of the DORA system, we can clearly see that the competition among top AI models has shifted from pure "algorithm design" to "systems engineering." Being able to stably run asynchronous RL algorithms on tens of thousands of graphics cards is itself an extremely high technical barrier. This also implies that future large model R&D will further concentrate on giants with ultra-large-scale computing clusters.

### 7.3 Future Challenges

Despite LongCat's excellent performance, the high computational cost of Heavy Thinking mode remains a major obstacle to practical application. How to further reduce latency while maintaining reasoning depth may be the focus of the next stage of research. Additionally, although ZigZag Attention optimizes video memory, reasoning processing million-level contexts is still expensive.
